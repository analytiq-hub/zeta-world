Zeta World

The Riemann zeta function, explained, and the announced proof of the Riemann hypothesis, visualized.

This landscape is the size of the Riemann zeta function, which encodes how the prime numbers are spread out. Each green dot is a zero, where the land drops to height zero. The Riemann hypothesis says that all of them, infinitely many, sit on the white line.

Status, 22 Nov 2026: a computer-checked proof of the Riemann hypothesis was released on 20 Nov and certified correct on 21 Nov in a joint statement of the Lean and Mathlib communities. Its author has published it only as Lean code, with no paper yet, and it has not been peer reviewed. This page shows what the statement says. It proves nothing.

Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom. The 3D view needs WebGL.

How to read it The ground is the complex plane s = σ + it, for σ from −1 to 2 and t from 10 to 50. Height is log(1 + |ζ(s)|), so zeros touch the ground. Color is the phase (angle) of ζ(s): going once around a zero passes through every color. Faint bands mark each doubling of |ζ|. The white line is σ = ½, the middle of the critical strip (σ from 0 to 1, between the gray lines), which holds every nontrivial zero. Open the explorer to move along t, switch to ξ(s), or probe the critical line.
